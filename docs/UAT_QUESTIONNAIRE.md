# LearnSync user acceptance questionnaire

## Session setup

Use a disposable LearnSync environment and dedicated dummy accounts. Invite 2–3 participants representing administration, faculty, and students. Test one participant at a time. Do not record passwords, real student information, or other sensitive data.

Tell each participant: “We are testing the system, not you. Please work through each task as you normally would. I will not guide you unless you ask to stop.”

Give each task as written, without naming a page, menu, or button. Start a timer when the task is read. Stop after three minutes. Record the participant’s result before moving to the next task.

The provided **Lesson 5: Results and Discussion** PDF is instructional material for the faculty and student tasks. Use it as a lesson resource, not as a prospectus or curriculum import file.

## Administrator tasks

1. “Set up the coming semester for the school.”
2. “Add the subjects from the curriculum prospectus you were given.”
3. “Create a section called 1A and assign a teacher to its subject.”
4. “Add the students from the supplied student list to section 1A. One row names a section that does not exist.”
5. “Find an invited person who has not accepted yet and resend their invitation.”
6. “A student says their account is locked. Find the account, deactivate it, then make it active again.”

## Faculty tasks

1. “Accept your invitation and choose a password.”
2. “Find out what needs your attention today.”
3. “Grade the three activity submissions and give one student feedback.”
4. “Make the results visible to students.”
5. “Write an announcement for section 1A only.”
6. “Publish the Midterm grade for your class and download it as a PDF.”
7. “Publish the provided Results and Discussion PDF as a lesson for the appropriate class.”

## Student tasks

1. “Accept your invitation and choose a password.”
2. “Find the next thing you should do.”
3. “Find everything due this week across your subjects.”
4. “Open the Results and Discussion lesson, mark it complete, and ask Study Help to explain one idea from it in simpler words.”
5. “Take the quiz, then find your result and Midterm grade.”
6. “Report a problem with the page you are on.”

For task 4, the facilitator may check after the task whether the student understood the PDF’s distinction between reporting results and interpreting them in discussion. Do not give the participant that distinction in the task prompt.

## Follow-up questions

Ask each participant:

1. What was the most confusing moment?
2. Was there anything you expected to find but could not?
3. How easy was it to know what to do next? (1 = very difficult, 5 = very easy)
4. What is the one change that would make the system easier for you?

## Review and sign-off

Summarize repeated confusion, incomplete tasks, and requested changes by role. Distinguish participant feedback from verified defects. Escalate any data loss, permission-boundary failure, exposed private information, or assessment answer exposure as a blocker. Record unresolved items and the basis for acceptance; do not claim UAT passed if no participants completed the tasks.
